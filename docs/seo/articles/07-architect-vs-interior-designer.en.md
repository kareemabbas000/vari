# Architect, Interior Architect or Interior Designer: Who Do You Need?

The three titles overlap enough to confuse almost everyone, including people who hire them regularly. The short version: an **architect** designs buildings and is responsible for their structure, safety and approval. An **interior designer** shapes the inside of spaces: layout, finishes, furniture, light and atmosphere. An **interior architect** sits between the two, working on interiors with a deeper reach into construction, such as moving walls, new stairs and building services.

Which one you need depends on one question: **what is your project going to change?**

## Start with what changes

**A new building or an extension.** You need an architect. New structures require design that meets building regulations, structural coordination and, in most places, a permit application signed by a qualified professional.

**Structural changes inside an existing building.** Removing a load-bearing wall, adding a mezzanine, changing a staircase or rerouting services means an architect or an interior architect, plus a structural engineer. Many jurisdictions require approval for this kind of work.

**A fit-out within an existing shell.** A new apartment or office being finished inside a completed building is the natural territory of interior architects and interior designers: layout, kitchens, bathrooms, joinery, lighting, finishes.

**A refresh.** New finishes, furniture, colour and lighting without touching the structure. An interior designer is usually the right choice.

Many projects need more than one of these. A villa needs an architect for the building and an interior designer for the rooms, and the result is better when they talk early.

## What each one actually does

**Architects** take a project from brief and site through concept, design development and technical drawings to permits and, often, supervision on site. They coordinate engineers and are accountable for compliance with building regulations.

**Interior architects** design interior spaces as constructed environments: spatial planning, partitions, ceilings, stairs, lighting and services coordination, with technical drawings that contractors build from.

**Interior designers** focus on how a space looks, feels and works for the people in it: layout, materials, colour, furniture, textiles, lighting and styling. Many also manage purchasing and installation.

## The legal lines differ by country

Professional titles are regulated differently around the world, so check the rules where your project is:

- **Germany.** "Architekt" and "Innenarchitekt" are protected professional titles under the architects' laws of the federal states. Only people registered with a state chamber of architects (Architektenkammer) may use them.
- **United Kingdom.** The title "architect" is protected by law. Only people registered with the Architects Registration Board may use it.
- **United States.** Architects are licensed by each state. Interior design regulation varies widely: some states regulate the title or the practice, many do not.

Elsewhere, ask your local professional body or the authority that issues building permits who may design and sign the kind of work you plan.

## How fees usually work

Architects often quote a percentage of construction cost, a fixed fee per stage, or hourly rates. In Germany, the official fee regulation for architects and engineers (HOAI) is a common reference. Since 2021 its rates have been guidance rather than binding minimums. Interior architects and designers typically charge fixed fees per project or per room, hourly rates, a fee per square metre, or a combination. Some add a margin on furniture they purchase for you.

Whichever model you choose, ask for the scope in writing: which stages are included, how many design options, how many revisions, and whether site visits and purchasing are part of it.

## FAQ

**Can an interior designer remove walls?**
Not on their own if the wall is load-bearing or the change needs approval. That requires an architect or interior architect and a structural engineer, and often a permit. Non-structural partitions are a different matter, but check local rules.

**Is an interior architect a real architect?**
In some countries, such as Germany, "Innenarchitekt" is a protected title with its own registration. Elsewhere the term is used more loosely. Check the person's registration where your project is.

**Who is cheaper, an architect or an interior designer?**
It depends on scope. For a refresh, an interior designer is usually more economical. For structural work or a new building you need an architect regardless, and a good one saves money by avoiding costly mistakes.

**Do I need both an architect and an interior designer?**
For new buildings and major renovations, often yes. The architect handles the building and approvals, the interior designer the spaces people live and work in. The best results come when they collaborate from the start.
