# The Best Architectural Visualization Studios, and How to Choose the Right One

Search for the best architectural visualization studios and you will mostly find lists written by visualization studios, many of which place themselves first. We are a visualization studio too, so we have tried to write the list we would want to read: one with a rule for who gets in, a source for every claim, and no pretence that one studio is best for every project.

Our rule is simple. A studio appears here only if it has been recognised by an independent publication or an industry award, not by another studio's ranking. We group them by what they are known for rather than numbering them, because a competition jury, a luxury developer and a city planning department need very different things from an image. Facts were last checked on 1 November 2026.

## Studios known for the image as an argument

Some studios are famous less for volume than for a point of view. Their images are recognisable before you read the credit.

**MIR** (Norway). Founded in 2000, MIR is known for atmospheric images set firmly in their landscape, with dramatic light, a distinctive colour palette and often few or no people. That is how ArchDaily described the studio in its 2020 feature on distinguished visualization studios. MIR's work is a reference point for anyone who believes a render should feel like a place rather than a product shot.

**Luxigon** (Paris). Luxigon says it has produced thousands of images, films, real-time 3D and VR files over the past twenty years, for architects, real estate companies and public institutions, including competition entries. ArchDaily's 2020 feature noted its abstract, dramatic and highly saturated imagery for competitions and conceptual projects, and listed offices in Paris, Los Angeles and Milan at the time. Its current site lists a Paris address.

What this group offers is authorship. What it asks of a client is trust: you are commissioning a vision, not only an illustration.

## Studios built for complete campaigns

Larger projects, a tower launch or a masterplan, often need more than images: films, interactive sales tools, sometimes the project's branding. A few studios have organised themselves around exactly that.

**Brick Visual** (Budapest, Cluj-Napoca, Porto). Brick Visual describes itself as "a creative and technology partner for real estate and global brands", covering still imagery, film and animation, real-time and VR sales tools, and project branding. At the 2024 CGArchitect 3D Awards it won both Studio of the Year and Best Commissioned Image. ArchDaily's 2020 feature also mentioned its training arm, Brick Academy.

**DBOX** (Miami, New York, London, Budapest, Lviv). DBOX presents itself as a full-service strategic consultancy that builds brand campaigns for design- and luxury-driven projects across residential, commercial, hospitality and cultural work. Its film work won Best Commissioned Film at the 2018 CGArchitect 3D Awards.

**Kilograph**. Kilograph calls itself a global narrative strategy and creative agency. Its work spans brand strategy and identity, websites and interactive experiences, films and visualization, largely for infrastructure, civic, institutional and commercial clients. It won the Interactive category at the 2018 CGArchitect 3D Awards.

This group suits projects where the images are one part of a larger sales or communication programme. It is usually overkill for a single façade study.

## A studio known for longevity

**Hayes Davidson** (London). Founded in 1989, Hayes Davidson was described by ArchDaily as one of the longest-running visualization studios and the first of its kind in the UK, with realistic imagery in a polished, optimistic style. In a young discipline, three decades of continuous practice is itself a credential.

## Where to look for who is doing the best work right now

Lists age quickly; studios change, people move. If you want to know who is doing outstanding work this year, the most useful independent signal is the **CGArchitect 3D Awards**, held every year, and published features in architecture media such as ArchDaily. Treat any list, including this one, as a starting point for your own shortlist, not as a verdict.

## How to choose for your project

The best studio in the world can still be the wrong studio for you. These questions usually decide it:

**What will the images do?** A competition entry rewards a strong point of view. An off-plan launch rewards accuracy across a whole series, because buyers compare views with plans and, later, with the finished building (we wrote about that in [Renders vs Reality](/en/blog/renders-vs-reality)). A planning submission rewards neutrality.

**Does the portfolio match your building?** A studio with superb interiors may not be the right one for a 40-storey tower, and the reverse. Look for completed series of projects similar to yours, viewed at full size.

**How will you work together?** Time-zone overlap, a named point of contact, clear approval stages and written revision terms matter as much as talent. Our [10-point checklist for choosing a rendering studio](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-3d-rendering-studio) goes through them one by one.

**What is the real budget?** Studios recognised by awards and media generally price accordingly. Our [rendering cost guide](/en/blog/architectural-rendering-cost) explains what moves the price per image.

**Do you need images, or a campaign?** If you need film, interactive sales tools and branding from one team, the campaign studios above are built for it. If you need a series of excellent stills and a walkthrough, a smaller studio may serve you better and closer.

## FAQ

**Who is the best architectural visualization studio in the world?**
There is no single answer, and lists that name one are usually written by that studio. Studios such as MIR, Brick Visual, Hayes Davidson and Luxigon are repeatedly recognised in independent publications and awards, but the best choice depends on what your images need to do.

**How do I compare visualization studio portfolios?**
View full-size images, ask for a complete series from one project rather than a single hero image, and look for work similar to your building type. Consistency across a series says more than one spectacular render.

**Are award-winning studios worth the premium?**
For competition entries, landmark projects and large launches, often yes. For a small residential project or a planning image, a studio that matches your scale, budget and time zone may give you better value.

**Can a smaller studio deliver the same quality?**
On individual images, often yes. Large studios add capacity, multidisciplinary teams and campaign services. Judge by portfolio series and process, not by size.
